    Staffing agencies lose hours daily to manual prospecting. Kurrent replaced that with a 10-stage automated intelligence engine: it found companies actively hiring, enriched each with firmographic data, surfaced the decision-makers, and delivered everything as white-label, client-branded intelligence briefings: daily digest, PDF reports, and a full client portal for working the pipeline.

    Reject what can’t be verified. The contact waterfall dropped any record it couldn’t confirm, trading lead volume for deliverability, so every name a recruiter opened was one they could act on.

    Impediment Intelligence

    Wavelo (Tucows)
    Award-winning AI platform, architected and engineered end-to-end

    Interactive demoAI award winnerData platformClassification

    Delivery for a major account generated enormous operational data: every blocker, dependency, and delay, all of it sitting unused. I saw the opportunity: turn that record into intelligence. I architected and engineered a platform that captured, classified, and attributed every impediment, revealing exactly where time went: internally, with the client, or with their partners.

    Recurring blockers were engineered out before they resurfaced, client conversations ran on shared evidence, and delivery decisions were made on data instead of memory. The CTOs of Tucows' three business units selected it as winner of the company's inaugural AI competition.

    The demo above is a generic reconstruction of the pattern on fictional data: Jira, Slack and GitHub evidence, classified and attributed, ending in the list of what to fix.

    The call

    Attribute to cause, never to a person. Attributing blockers to teams would have been cheaper to build and easier to read, and nobody would have used it. Cause and control kept adoption, and moved the client conversation from argument to evidence.

    Delivery framework, extended company-wide

    Every high-growth platform eventually meets enterprise expectations. I implemented and led the delivery framework that met them: five gated stages, dual sign-off, artifacts that made what, why, and how legible to anyone in the organization. Delivery for the company's largest account became predictable and on time, and leadership extended the framework across the company.

    The rule was simple: if it isn’t agreed, it isn’t built. Discovery aligned client and team on the problem; definition and design put the solution and its architecture through approval; build ran to deploy and a warranty period before anything moved on. Product learned the services they were speccing by writing acceptance tests first. Rework stopped, the QA backlog cleared, and the company adopted the model.

    04

    How a scoped build runs

    STEP 1

    Scope

    We define the build before anything gets built: the need, the v1 that proves it, the boundaries, and what handoff looks like. You know what you're getting, when it lands, and what it costs, before work begins.

    STEP 2

    Architect and build

    I design the architecture and build the working version: functional software your team can put in front of real users, not a deck about what could be built. Two to six weeks, fixed scope.

    STEP 3

    Hand off

    Your team takes ownership, with the documentation, the context, and the reasoning behind every architectural decision. The engagement ends by design, and the product doesn't need me to keep running.

    The fractional seat follows a simpler arc: a short scoping conversation, a three-month start, and a standing weekly cadence from there.

    05

    Terms for scoped builds

    Fixed scope, fixed price

    Priced to the scope and agreed before work begins. No surprises invoiced later.

    Start with a scoping sprint

    One week, fixed price: the architecture, the plan, and a firm quote for the build. If you stop there, you keep the plan.

    Two to six weeks

    The end date is set at the start, and the engagement is built to reach it.

    Half at start, half at handoff

    Simple, and it keeps both of us committed to the same finish line.

    One build at a time

    And one fractional product-leadership seat, separately. Never more than one of each.

    Handoff is the deliverable

    Documentation, context, and the reasoning behind every decision transfer with the product. Your team owns it outright.

    No retainers. No operational management

    If you need more after handoff, we scope a new engagement. We don't drift into one.
